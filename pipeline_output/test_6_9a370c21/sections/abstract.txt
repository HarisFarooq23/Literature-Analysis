The increasing demand for energy, combined with the environmental consequences of fossil-fuel consumption, has created a need for cleaner and renewable sources of energy.
Biofuels are renewable fuels produced from biological materials such as crops, agricultural residues, algae, and organic waste. Common biofuels include bioethanol, biodiesel, and
biogas. This paper examines the production, advantages, limitations, and environmental implications of biofuels as alternatives to conventional petroleum-based fuels. First-
generation biofuels are primarily produced from food crops, while second- and third-generation biofuels utilize lignocellulosic biomass, agricultural waste, and algae. Although
biofuels can reduce greenhouse-gas emissions and dependence on fossil fuels, their large-scale adoption is affected by issues including land use, food security, production costs,
and technological limitations. The analysis suggests that advanced biofuels produced from waste materials and non-food biomass have greater long-term potential than
conventional crop-based biofuels.
Keywords: Biofuels, biodiesel, bioethanol, renewable energy, biomass, sustainability, greenhouse gases
