The aviation industry faces an urgent need for sustainable fuels to 
mitigate climate change. The negative environmental impacts of fossil- 
based fuels, coupled with their rapid depletion and volatile market 
prices, have intensified global efforts to develop clean and renewable 
energy alternatives. The aviation sector faces growing challenges as air 
travel demand continues to rise, leading to increasing CO2 emissions 
that significantly contribute to climate change (Adzahar et al., 2026). 
Bio-jet fuel (BJF), composed primarily of C8-C16 hydrocarbons, has 
emerged as a promising sustainable alternative. BJF cuts life-cycle 
greenhouse gases while maintaining drop-in compatibility, in addition 
to lower sulfur content, lower tailpipe emissions, high thermal stability, 
and favourable cold-flow properties (Hebish et al., 2024). Triglyceride 
oils represent attractive feedstocks for BJF production, as their long- 
chain fatty acids closely resemble hydrocarbons. However, upgrading 
and oxygen removal are required, typically achieved through deoxy­
genation (DO) and hydrodeoxygenation (HDO). HDO relies on sub­
stantial amounts of high-pressure hydrogen gas, which raises safety 
concerns over storage, delivery and operational hazards and undermines 
sustainability, since clean H2 production is far from commercialization 
and thus approximately 96% of global hydrogen is currently derived 
from non-renewable sources such as naphtha natural gas reforming. In 
contrast, DO does not require external H2 input; instead, oxygen is 
removed through decarboxylation (CO2 release) or decarbonylation (CO 
and H2O release) collectively referred to as DeCox pathways, offering a 
more sustainable H2-free pathway for BJF production. Note, CO and CO2 
gases by-products of DO reaction can be captured and not released into 
the environment. On an industrial or pilot scale with a complete system, 
these gases will be recycled back and used as feedstocks to produce other 
valuable chemicals and intermediates such as methane, urea, syngas etc 
(James et al., 2023; Zhang et al., 2020). Several studies have reported on 
the promising potential of DO reactions. For instance, Why et al., 2022
reported a 96% liquid yield and 73% jet fuel selectivity for the DO of 
palm kernel oil under Pd/C. In another study, Asikin-Mijan et al., 2022
observed 77% hydrocarbon yield and 64% jet fuel selectivity for the DO 
of tung oil under CoW/SiO2-Al2O3. Furthermore, the DO of soybean oil 
under NbOPO4 was found to yield 91% liquid product and 58% jet-range 
selectivity (Scaldaferri and Pasa, 2019). Despite these advances, catalyst 
efficiency and energy consumption remain critical challenges.
Waste Cooking Oil (WCO) is a major byproduct of food preparation, 
and improper disposal poses serious environmental hazards. Globally, 
around 16.5 million tonnes of WCO are generated annually, creating 
challenges for environmentally safe disposal (Sharma et al., 2021). 
Palm-based WCO, comprising mainly of palmitic acid (C16:0) and oleic 
acid (C18:1), presents an attractive feedstock to produce BJF. In fact, 
WCO can produce highly competitive fuel and potentially most cost- 
effective, along with avoiding disposal hazards and avoiding direct 
competition with edible oil feedstocks in the food supply chain (Detsios 
et al., 2023).
Although noble metal catalysts exhibit excellent DO performance, 
their prohibitive cost and scarcity limit large-scale application (Hebish 
et al., 2024). Similarly, sulfided catalysts, despite their effectiveness, 
face challenges such as sulfur leaching and contamination of the final 
product, making them less desirable as catalysts for biofuel synthesis 
(Gamal et al., 2020). Consequently, this has driven the search for 
affordable, sulfur-free alternatives. In this regard, transition metals and 
their oxides have emerged as promising alternatives. Among them, Ni- 
based catalysts offer a sustainable and industrial relevant alternative 
in upgrading of triglyceride oils for biofuel production owing to their 
relatively low cost and scalability and demonstrated strong 
hydrogenation-DO capability and high catalytic activity (Aliana- 
Nasharuddin et al., 2020; Hafriz et al., 2022; Hartono et al., 2023; Khalit 
et al., 2021). As reported by Morgan et al., Ni showed higher DO and 
cracking activity than Pd/Pt on similar supports, yielding more C8-C17 
hydrocarbons (Morgan et al., 2010). Similar behaviour was observed by 
Jeon et al. during the DO of oleic acid over a Ni-CeZrO2 catalyst, which 
attained ~ 98% conversion C9-C18 hydrocarbon fraction of 34% and an 
oxygen removal efficiency of ~ 96% (Jeon et al., 2019). Furthermore, in 
a comparative study conducted by Hafriz and colleagues, transition 
metals Ni, Zn, Cu, Co and Fe were evaluated as catalyst promoters. 
Among these catalysts, findings reveal the Ni-based catalyst system 
exhibited the highest conversion activity (67%), high portion of satu­
rated hydrocarbons (80%), and highest selectivity towards green diesel 
(55%) and BJF (39%) (Hafriz et al., 2020). Collectively, these studies 
clearly demonstrate that Ni-promoted catalysts enhance DO activity. 
Among Ni-based catalyst, Ni supported on activated carbon (AC) shows 
great potential. Previously, Wu et al. performed DO on stearic acid over 
Ni/AC achieving an almost complete conversion with heptadecane (C17) 
selectivity of ~ 80% (Wu et al., 2016). The research group of (Khalit 
et al., 2021) tested Ni catalyst over various supports of AC, zeolite, 
and reduced graphene oxide for the DO of WCO to produce green diesel. 
In their work, Ni/AC was reported the most active catalyst by giving 
90% hydrocarbon yield and 89% selectivity towards n-(C15 + C17) owing 
to its good physicochemical properties of porosity, high surface area and 
high number of acid-base sites. It is noteworthy that, vanadium pent­
oxide (V2O5) is widely recognized as a cost-effective, thermally stable 
catalytic material with excellent redox and oxygen transfer properties 
that has great value in the industry (Marberger et al., 2019). Despite this, 
as far as we know the utilization of V2O5 support remains unexplored in 
DO reaction.
Recent studies show promising results in microwave irradiation (M) 
and sonication (S)-assisted synthesis techniques of active catalysts for 
biofuel upgrading, whereby these treatments resulted in enhanced 
physicochemical properties and catalytic performance (Luo et al., 2022). 
M method induces volumetric heating that leads to uniform temperature 
distribution, accelerated nucleation and improved metal dispersion on 
support. Furthermore, atoms movement occurs where angles of atoms 
will shift and open up the active sites. It is also reported that catalyst 
exhibited more porosity and higher surface area following microwave 
exposure (Burakova et al., 2018). For instance, Xue et al. reported the 
development of Ni2P/SiO2 catalysts using M method and the result 
shows enhanced dispersion of Ni2P particles on support and catalytic 
performance in the HDO of methyl palmitate (Xue et al., 2015). Simi­
larly, ultrasonic approaches exploit acoustic cavitation to induce local­
ised hotspots and micro-mixing resulting in enhanced catalyst surface 
properties and metal dispersion and thus improve catalytic activity (Li 
et al., 2021). In a study conducted by (Ameen et al., 2019) Ni-Mo/ 
γ-Al2O3 catalyst synthesis by ultrasound achieved significantly higher 
hydrocarbon yields (80.87%) during the HDO of rubber seed oil 
compared to conventional prepared catalyst (63.3%).
To the best of the authors’ knowledge, no systematic study has 
directly compared M and S treatments of the same catalyst formulation 
for triglyceride DO with focus on jet-fuel range hydrocarbons. Further­
more, although Ni-based catalysts have been extensively studied and 
V2O5 exhibits excellent potential as a catalyst support, the Ni/V2O5 
bimetallic catalyst remains largely unexplored, despite its potential 
synergistic effects that could significantly enhance catalytic activity.

Due to this reason, hence the present study, we developed a series of 
inexpensive Ni/V2O5 and compare with performance of established 
effective Ni/AC catalysts, using different synthesis treatments (conven­
tional, microwave and sonication), for the effective DO of WCO into 
biojet fuel product. To evaluate the effect of the treatments, the catalysts 
properties were studied through various characterizations, and the 
catalytic performance was assessed by analysing their respective liquid 
product with regards to BJF selectivity. Additionally, kinetic deactiva­
tion study was also carried out to provide insights into catalyst deacti­
vation to assess the catalyst further.
