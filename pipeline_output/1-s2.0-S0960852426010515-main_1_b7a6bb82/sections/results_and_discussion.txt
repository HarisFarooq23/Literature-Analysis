3.1. Characterization of synthesised catalysts
XRD patterns (Fig. S2a-b) confirm orthorhombic V2O5 (JCPDS card 
no 01–075-9978), and nickel vanadate phases such as Ni3(VO4), Ni 
(V2O6) and Ni2(V2O7) in all Ni/V2O5 catalysts. The absence of distinct 
metallic Ni or NiO indicates the formation of strong Nickel Vanadium 
Oxide interactions during synthesis and calcination, producing mixed 
oxide phases instead of separate Ni or NiO domains, this is in line with 
previous reports where incorporation of Ni into oxide supports sup­
presses Ni and NiO reflections in XRD (Adzahar et al., 2026). In the case 
of Ni/AC catalysts result reveals sharp reflections indexed to metallic Ni 
(FCC, JCPDS card no. 00–004-0850) implying a greater proportion of 
crystalline Ni0 sites along with the broad band of graphitic carbon at 
about 2θ = 26◦. Trace NiO (JCPDS card no. 01–078-0643) is only 
detected in the C sample, whereas both M and S exhibit exclusively 
metallic Ni, implying that M and S treatments favour full reduction of Ni 
species during synthesis likely due to the rapid heating and accelerated 
nucleation of M and S-assistance. This observation was previously 
confirmed by Lim et al., 2021 from using H2-TPR where they reported 
that sonicated catalysts display more reducible NiO, indicating a higher 
fraction of Ni can be reduced to metallic Ni (Ni0). A similar outcome for 
microwaved catalysts was revealed by (Aimdate et al., 2021). Further­
more, the crystallite size was determined at the most intense peak at 2θ 
= 44.5◦(Ni) and 2θ = 20.4◦(V2O5) for Ni/AC and Ni/V2O5 catalysts, 
respectively (Table 3). In terms of Ni/AC catalysts, crystallite size fol­
lowed the decreasing trend S > C > M, suggesting that M method 
improved Ni dispersion and availability of active sites. Surprisingly, the 
crystallite size of Ni on Ni/AC obtained from S method was the largest 
(24 nm) among the rest AC supported catalyst. Former literature reports 
that for carbon-based supports dispersion quality depends on sonication 
energy density, too low energy leaves large agglomerates and too high 
causes re-agglomeration, thereby increasing crystallite size (Rennhofer 
and Zanghellini, 2021). For Ni/V2O5 catalyst crystallite size decreased 
in the order C > S > M, revealing greater dispersion by M system. Note, C 
method for both catalyst series showed to favour the formation of large 
crystallite size and reduced dispersion. Remarkably, the notable 
decrease in crystallinity for Ni/V2O5 M suggests greater dispersion 
resulting in finer crystallites and greater lattice imperfections, which can 
be attributed to the rapid disorderly atom arrangement due to micro­
wave volumetric heating, therefore promoting the generation of more 
interfacial Ni-O-V sites which are reported to exhibit Ni2+/Ni3+ and 
V5+/V4+ redox couples with lattice oxygen participation and likely 
originated from Ni3(VO4), Ni(V2O6) and Ni2(V2O7) phases (Li et al., 
2020). Such redox-active sites are known to promote C-–O bond 
cleavage and oxygen removal facilitating DeCOx pathways (Song et al., 
2023).
BET analysis (Table 3, Fig. S2c-f) shows synthesis methods strongly 
influence surface area and porosity. As anticipated, the Ni/AC catalyst 
series exhibited the highest surface area, owing to the inherent porous 
structure of carbon. The BET surface area revealed was ~ 0.71–––2.56 
m2/g and 918.95–––997.87 m2/g for Ni/V2O5 and Ni/AC series, 
respectively. As expected, V2O5 crystallizes in dense layered structures 
which limits the formation of pores and results in low surface area 
(often < 5 m2/g) (Marberger et al., 2019). Interestingly, Ni/V2O5 M 
demonstrated the ability to retain a surface area close to that of the V2O5 
support, suggesting microwave improved dispersion compared to C and 
S methods. Conversely, S methods resulted in detrimental surface area 
and porosity, likely due to agglomeration caused by ultrasonic wave 
cavitation. The N2 adsorption–desorption test shown in Fig. S2(c) car­
ried out shows Ni/V2O5 series represented a hysteresis type III H3 
characteristic of slit-shaped pores which was corroborated by SEM im­
ages. In contrast, Ni/AC series displayed a hysteresis type IV H4 (Fig. S2
(d)) linked to microporous materials with narrow slit-like pores, as 
confirmed in SEM images (see Fig. S5c-d). Indeed, this finding in 
agreement with XRD findings, whereby the M method capable of 
improving the metal dispersion, preserving surface area and enhancing 
active site accessibility. As been noted before (Horv´ath et al., 2024),
